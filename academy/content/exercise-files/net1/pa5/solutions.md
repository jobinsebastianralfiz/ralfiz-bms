# Solutions: Practice set 2.1

**1. Answer: (2)**
The study solves a practical, immediate problem for a decision-maker: applied research. It does not aim at theory (fundamental), is not about the past (historical), and uses data, not only ideas (conceptual). It is not action research either, because the officer is commissioning outsiders rather than improving her own practice in cycles.

**2. Answer: (3)**
Research is self-correcting: findings are provisional and are revised when better evidence appears. “Final and beyond revision” is the opposite of the scientific attitude.

**3. Answer: (4)**
Comte: law of three stages (III). Popper: falsifiability (I). Kuhn: paradigm shift (IV). Glaser and Strauss: grounded theory (II). Option (2) swaps Popper and Kuhn.

**4. Answer: (3)**
The “treatment” (medium of instruction) happened before the study and was not assigned by the researcher; existing records are compared. That is ex post facto (causal-comparative). Quasi-experimental would need the researcher to apply a treatment to intact groups.

**5. Answer: (1)**
Both are true, and R explains A: because each method is fallible, post-positivists combine methods, sources or investigators so that their biases do not all point the same way.

**6. Answer: (2)**
Authenticity of the document itself (age, material, authorship) is external criticism. Internal criticism would ask whether its content is accurate.

**7. Answer: (1)**
Both are correct definitions of longitudinal designs. The panel study is the one that follows the same individuals.

**8. Answer: (3)**
With random assignment: posttest-only control group (A), Solomon four-group (C) and pretest–posttest control group (E) are true experiments. The one-group pretest–posttest (B) and static-group comparison (D) are pre-experimental.

**9. Answer: (2)**
The verification principle (a statement is meaningful only if it can be verified empirically, or is true by definition) is the hallmark of logical positivism.

**10. Answer: (4)**
Plan, act, observe, reflect: B, D, C, A. Then a new cycle begins with a revised plan.

**11. Answer: (4)**
Correlation shows association, not causation, so Statement I is false. Correlational research describes relationships as they exist, so it belongs to the descriptive family: Statement II is true.

**12. Answer: (2)**
Phenomenology: lived experience (III). Ethnography: culture through immersion (I). Grounded theory: theory from data (II). Narrative research: life stories (IV).

**13. Answer: (3)**
Qualitative first, to explore and build an instrument, then a quantitative phase: exploratory sequential. Explanatory sequential is the reverse order.

**14. Answer: (4)**
Verstehen (A), socially constructed realities (B) and idiographic understanding (D) are interpretivist. A value-free observer (C) and universal laws (E) are positivist.

**15. Answer: (1)**
Both are true, and R gives the reason: manipulation establishes that the cause comes first, control removes other factors, and random assignment equalises the groups, so rival explanations are ruled out.
