# Practice set 6.1: Arguments and deductive/inductive reasoning

Time yourself: problems 1–8 in 12 minutes, problems 9–15 in 12 minutes. Every question has four options; choose one.

**1.** “No saints are liars. Some priests are liars. Therefore, some priests are not saints.” The mood and figure are:
(1) EIO-1  (2) EIO-2  (3) IEO-2  (4) EIO-4

**2.** “Some scientists are not philosophers. All philosophers are thinkers. Therefore, some thinkers are not scientists.” Which rule does it break?
(1) Undistributed middle  (2) Illicit major  (3) Illicit minor  (4) Exclusive premises

**3.** In the traditional square, “Some S are P” is false. Then:
(1) “All S are P” is true  (2) “No S is P” is undetermined  (3) “No S is P” is true and “Some S are not P” is true  (4) “Some S are not P” is false

**4.** Match List I (immediate inference) with List II (result).

| List I | List II |
|---|---|
| A. Converse of “No P is Q” | I. Some Q are P |
| B. Obverse of “All P are Q” | II. Some non-Q are not non-P |
| C. Contrapositive of “Some P are not Q” | III. No Q is P |
| D. Converse by limitation of “All P are Q” | IV. No P is non-Q |

(1) A-III, B-IV, C-II, D-I  (2) A-III, B-II, C-IV, D-I  (3) A-I, B-IV, C-II, D-III  (4) A-III, B-IV, C-I, D-II

**5.** Assertion (A): The syllogism “No fish are mammals. No reptiles are fish. Therefore, no reptiles are mammals.” is invalid.
Reason (R): No valid categorical syllogism has two negative premises.
(1) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
(2) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A)
(3) (A) is true but (R) is false
(4) (A) is false but (R) is true

**6.** Statement I: Every sound argument is valid.
Statement II: Every valid argument is sound.
(1) Both Statement I and Statement II are true  (2) Both Statement I and Statement II are false  (3) Statement I is true but Statement II is false  (4) Statement I is false but Statement II is true

**7.** “You had better support the new policy; the director remembers who opposes him when promotions are decided.” The fallacy is:
(1) Ad misericordiam  (2) Ad populum  (3) Ad verecundiam  (4) Ad baculum

**8.** Which pairs of propositions (same terms, traditional square) cannot both be true?
A. A and E  B. A and O  C. I and O  D. E and I  E. A and I
(1) A, B and D only  (2) A and B only  (3) A, B, C and D only  (4) B and D only

**9.** Arrange from the widest denotation to the narrowest.
A. Metal  B. Substance  C. Iron  D. Element
(1) B, A, D, C  (2) B, D, A, C  (3) D, B, A, C  (4) C, A, D, B

**10.** “The notice said: ‘Wanted, a hall for a meeting of students with good ventilation.’ So the students must have good ventilation.” The fallacy is:
(1) Equivocation  (2) Accent  (3) Amphiboly  (4) Composition

**11.** Which of the following are inductive arguments?
A. An argument from analogy between two drugs
B. A generalisation from a sample survey
C. An argument in the form of modus ponens
D. A prediction about next year’s enrolment from past trends
E. A syllogism in Barbara
(1) A and B only  (2) A, B and D only  (3) B, D and E only  (4) A, B, C and D only

**12.** Counting the 24 traditionally valid syllogistic forms, how many belong to the first figure?
(1) 4  (2) 5  (3) 6  (4) 8

**13.** From “All A are B” and “No C is B”, which conclusion follows validly?
(1) All A are C  (2) No A is C  (3) Some C are A  (4) Some A are C

**14.** A researcher observes that as the dose of a fertiliser is raised step by step, crop yield rises in proportion. The inductive method used is:
(1) Agreement  (2) Difference  (3) Residues  (4) Concomitant variation

**15.** The obverse of “Some students are not punctual” is:
(1) Some punctual persons are not students  (2) Some students are non-punctual  (3) No students are punctual  (4) All students are non-punctual
