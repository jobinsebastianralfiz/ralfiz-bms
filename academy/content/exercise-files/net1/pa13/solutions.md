# Solutions 6.1: Arguments and deductive/inductive reasoning

**1. Answer: (2)**
Conclusion “Some priests are not saints”: S = priests, P = saints, M = liars. Major premise (with P): No saints are liars, E, P–M. Minor premise: Some priests are liars, I, S–M. Mood EIO; the middle term is the predicate of both premises, so figure 2 (Festino, valid). IEO-2 comes from not reordering.

**2. Answer: (2)**
Conclusion “Some thinkers are not scientists”: S = thinkers, P = scientists, M = philosophers. Major premise: Some scientists are not philosophers (O, P–M); minor: All philosophers are thinkers (A, M–S). Form OAO-4. The O conclusion distributes “scientists”, but in the major premise “scientists” is the subject of an O proposition and is undistributed: illicit major. The middle term is distributed (subject of the A premise), and only one premise is negative.

**3. Answer: (3)**
I false makes its contradictory E true. E true makes its subaltern O true and its contrary A false. All four are settled: A false, E true, I false, O true.

**4. Answer: (1)**
E converts simply: No Q is P (III). Obverse of A: No P is non-Q (IV). Contrapositive of O: Some non-Q are not non-P (II). A converts only by limitation: Some Q are P (I).

**5. Answer: (1)**
Two negative premises only say what is excluded from what; they establish no link between the minor and major terms through the middle term. Here fish are outside mammals and reptiles are outside fish; reptiles could still be anywhere with respect to mammals. R is the rule that makes A true.

**6. Answer: (3)**
Sound means valid with all premises true, so every sound argument is valid. A valid argument may have false premises (“All birds are mammals; all crows are birds; so all crows are mammals”), so it need not be sound.

**7. Answer: (4)**
The “reason” is a veiled threat about promotions, an appeal to force (ad baculum). It gives no evidence that the policy is good.

**8. Answer: (1)**
Contraries A–E cannot both be true; contradictories A–O and E–I cannot both be true. I and O (subcontraries) can both be true, and A and I are both true whenever A is true.

**9. Answer: (2)**
Every iron sample is a metal, every metal is an element, and every element is a substance, so the classes shrink in the order substance, element, metal, iron: B, D, A, C.

**10. Answer: (3)**
The ambiguity lies in the grammar: “with good ventilation” can attach to the hall or to the students. A loose construction that allows two readings is amphiboly. Equivocation needs one word used in two senses.

**11. Answer: (2)**
Analogy, sampling generalisation and prediction from trends all give conclusions that are only probable: inductive. Modus ponens and Barbara are deductive forms.

**12. Answer: (3)**
Figure 1 has four unconditionally valid forms (Barbara AAA, Celarent EAE, Darii AII, Ferio EIO) and two conditionally valid ones (AAI and EAO), 6 in all. Every figure has exactly 6 of the 24.

**13. Answer: (2)**
B is the middle term. All A lie inside B, and C lies wholly outside B, so no A is C (Camestres pattern). The E conclusion distributes A and C, and each is distributed in its premise (A in the A premise, C in the E premise). “Some C are A” contradicts the valid conclusion.

**14. Answer: (4)**
Two quantities varying together in step is the method of concomitant variation.

**15. Answer: (2)**
Obversion changes the quality (O to I) and replaces the predicate by its complement: “Some students are non-punctual”. (1) is the converse of O, which is invalid.
