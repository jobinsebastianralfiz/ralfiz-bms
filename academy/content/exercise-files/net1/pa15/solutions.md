# Solutions 6.3: Indian logic

**1. Answer: (3)**
The Bhāṭṭas (and Advaitins) hold that an absence is known by the non-apprehension of what would be perceived if present: anupalabdhi. Nyāya would say the absence is perceived (pratyakṣa); Prābhākaras reject anupalabdhi.

**2. Answer: (1)**
Nyāya: four (II). Prābhākara: four + arthāpatti (III). Bhāṭṭa: all six (IV). Sāṅkhya: perception, inference, testimony (I).

**3. Answer: (1)**
Thesis (pratijñā), reason (hetu), example (udāharaṇa), application (upanaya), conclusion (nigamana): E, B, D, C, A.

**4. Answer: (2)**
The hetu, smoke, is not present in the pakṣa, the lake. A reason absent from its own subject is svarūpāsiddha, a kind of asiddha.

**5. Answer: (1)**
When the pakṣa is “all things”, there is nothing outside it to serve as a positive or negative instance, so the vyāpti cannot be checked and the reason is inconclusive: anupasaṃhārī. R gives exactly this reason.

**6. Answer: (3)**
Statement I is the Nyāya account of upamiti (saṃjñā–saṃjñi-sambandha). Statement II is false: Vaiśeṣika accepts only perception and inference and brings comparison under inference.

**7. Answer: (2)**
The negative form, absence of the sādhya with absence of the hetu, is vyatireka vyāpti. The positive “wherever smoke, there fire” is anvaya vyāpti.

**8. Answer: (2)**
The five conditions are pakṣadharmatā, sapakṣasattva, vipakṣāsattva, abādhitaviṣayatva and asatpratipakṣatva. Savyabhicāra is a fallacy, not a condition.

**9. Answer: (3)**
From an effect (flood) to its cause (rain): śeṣavat.

**10. Answer: (2)**
Audibility belongs to sound alone. It occurs in the pakṣa but in no other case, positive or negative, so no vyāpti can be established: asādhāraṇa (too narrow). Option (1) describes sādhāraṇa.

**11. Answer: (2)**
Pramā is valid knowledge (III), pramātā the knower (I), prameya the object (II), pramāṇa the means (IV).

**12. Answer: (3)**
Perception, inference, comparison, testimony and postulation (arthāpatti): five.

**13. Answer: (2)**
A past cognition of fragrance presents the quality in the present perception: jñānalakṣaṇa. Sāmānyalakṣaṇa is perception of a whole class through its universal; yogaja is yogic.

**14. Answer: (3)**
Pratijñā and nigamana state the same thesis at the start and the end, so Statement I is true. Aristotle’s syllogism has only two premises and a conclusion, with no separate example: Statement II is false.

**15. Answer: (4)**
The words are complete, uttered together and intended, but fire cannot be used to water anything: the meanings are incompatible, so yogyatā is missing.
