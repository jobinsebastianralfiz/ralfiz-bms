# Solutions: Practice set 1.1

**1. (3)** Reflective level – Hunt is correct. Memory level is Herbart’s, understanding level is Morrison’s, and Jackson gave the phases of teaching, not a level.

**2. (1)** Knowledge, Comprehension (B), Application (E), Analysis (D), Synthesis (A), Evaluation (C). In the 1956 version Synthesis is below Evaluation; only the 2001 revision puts Create on top. Option 2 applies the revised order to the old names, which is wrong for this question.

**3. (2)** “Compare” is one of the Understand processes in the revised taxonomy (interpreting, exemplifying, classifying, summarising, inferring, comparing, explaining). If the objective had asked the student to judge which structure is better against criteria, it would be Evaluate.

**4. (1)** Memory – recall and recognition (A-II); Understanding – explanation and application (B-III); Reflective – open-ended problems and projects (C-I); Autonomous development – self-assessment of self-directed work (D-IV).

**5. (1)** Both are true, and R is why Create is at the top: creating a new product requires remembering, understanding, applying, analysing and evaluating.

**6. (3)** Statement I is a genuine maxim. Statement II is false: the Gestalt-based maxim is “from whole to part”; “part to whole” reverses it.

**7. (1)** Objectives (A), diagnosing entry behaviour (B) and choosing aids (D) are planning tasks before class. Reinforcing answers (C) is interactive; analysing test results (E) is post-active.

**8. (1)** Listening attentively is attending, the Receiving level. Responding would require active participation, such as asking questions or giving feedback.

**9. (1)** Perception (B), Set (E), Guided response (C), Mechanism (A), Complex overt response, Adaptation, Origination (D). Set (readiness to act) comes right after Perception.

**10. (2)** Gage: interpersonal influence aimed at changing behaviour potential. (1) is Morrison, (3) Burton, (4) B. O. Smith.

**11. (4)** A is false: teaching is an activity intended to produce learning; learning is the change in the learner. R is true: people learn from books, experience and practice without a teacher.

**12. (1)** 4 knowledge types × 6 processes = 24 cells. The metacognitive row is one knowledge type across all 6 processes, so 6 cells.

**13. (3)** Feedback 6 + re-feedback 6 = 12 minutes of 36, which is 12/36 = 1/3.

**14. (1)** Morrison proposed it (A); its aim is insight (C); assimilation is one of its steps (D). The learner is active, not passive (B wrong); hypothesis testing is the reflective level (E wrong).

**15. (1)** Physical books first is concrete to abstract; everyday stacking that students already know is known to unknown. The other options reverse genuine maxims.
