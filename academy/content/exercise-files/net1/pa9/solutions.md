# Solutions 4.1: Communication: meaning, types and barriers

**1. Answer: (2)**
Encoding converts an idea into symbols. Decoding is the receiver's interpretation; feedback is the response.

**2. Answer: (4)**
Oculesics = eyes (IV); olfactics = smell (III); artifactics = clothing and objects (II); paralanguage = vocal cues (I). A-IV, B-III, C-II, D-I.

**3. Answer: (1)**
Both are true. Because noise includes semantic and psychological interference (R), it can arise at encoding and decoding, not only in the channel (A). R explains A.

**4. Answer: (3)**
Statement I is the definition of horizontal communication. Statement II describes upward communication; downward communication carries instructions, policies and circulars.

**5. Answer: (2)**
C(10, 2) = 10 × 9 ÷ 2 = 45. 90 counts every channel twice; 9 is one member's links.

**6. Answer: (3)**
Ethnocentrism, stereotyping, different norms of eye contact and language differences are barriers. Seeking feedback (D) is a remedy, not a barrier.

**7. Answer: (2)**
Forming, storming, norming, performing: B, D, C, A.

**8. Answer: (3)**

**9. Answer: (4)**

**10. Answer: (3)**
The blind area holds what others know about you and you do not; feedback moves it into the open area. Self-disclosure reduces the hidden area.

**11. Answer: (2)**
Low-context communication is explicit and word-based. Options (1) and (3) describe high-context communication; (4) is false for low-context cultures, which rely heavily on written agreements.

**12. Answer: (4)**
In the wheel all messages pass through the centre, so it is the most centralised and is fast and accurate for simple tasks. The all-channel network is the least centralised.

**13. Answer: (1)**
Both are true, and R explains A: because the grapevine bypasses formal levels and procedures, it moves quickly.

**14. Answer: (3)**
Deliberately passing on only favourable information as a message moves up the hierarchy is filtering, an organisational barrier. The words are clear (not semantic), and nothing is wrong with the environment or the body.

**15. Answer: (2)**
Effective classroom communication is two-way: the teacher uses feedback to adjust. The other options remove feedback.
