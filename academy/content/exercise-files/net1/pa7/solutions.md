**1. Answer: (2)**
APA: surnames joined by “&” inside parentheses, a comma, the year, a comma and “p.”: (Sharma & Iyer, 2021, p. 12). Option (1) is MLA and option (3) is Chicago author–date.

**2. Answer: (3)**
The same source as the immediately preceding note is ibid.; the page changed, so it is “Ibid., 34”. Op. cit. is for a work cited earlier with other notes in between, and loc. cit. for the same page of such a work. (Chicago 17 itself prefers a shortened note, but the question asks for the traditional form.)

**3. Answer: (4)**

**4. Answer: (2)**

**5. Answer: (2)**

**6. Answer: (4)**
MLA places the date near the end, among the publication details (for example “vol. 48, no. 2, 2022”), not in parentheses after the author, which is APA. MLA does put article titles in quotation marks.

**7. Answer: (1)**
Verbatim copying (A), unattributed paraphrase (B), undisclosed self-reuse (C) and translation passed off as one’s own (E) are all plagiarism. Proper quotation with citation (D) is not.
