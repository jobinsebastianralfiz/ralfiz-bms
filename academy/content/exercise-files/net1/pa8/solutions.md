**1. Answer: (2)**
The passage argues that abundance of information makes attention scarce (P1), explains the business built on selling attention (P2), why harms are neglected (P3), the costs (P4) and the remedies (P5). Option (2) covers this. (1) is extreme: the passage never calls for a ban. (3) is a detail from P1 (too narrow). (4) contradicts “None of this requires anyone to intend harm”.

**2. Answer: (2)**
“their attention is the product, sold to advertisers in small measured units”. The advertisers are the paying customers. (1) and (3) are not in the passage; (4) reverses it (platforms want as much use as possible).

**3. Answer: (3)**
P3: “engagement is easy to measure, whereas the effect of a product on a person's concentration, sleep or relationships is not. What is measured tends to be managed.” The other options are invented.

**4. Answer: (4)**
P2 lists unpredictable notifications, endless feeds and learning systems. Paying users is never mentioned; in fact the services are free and earn from advertisers.

**5. Answer: (1)**
P4: engagement systems “may give it a prominence out of proportion to its accuracy or importance”. So prominence does not imply accuracy. (2) contradicts P5 (willpower is called “a weak defence” by some). (3) is extreme and out of scope. (4) is not stated; remedies are still “being debated”.

**6. Answer: (3)**
Weekday borrowing fell while weekend borrowing rose, so a shift to weekends is the best-supported inference. (1) is contradicted by the doubling at weekends. (2) cannot be determined: we do not know the base numbers (a third of a large weekday figure may exceed double a small weekend figure). (4) is a recommendation, not an inference.

**7. Answer: (2)**
The argument moves from a correlation (voluntary attenders learn more) to a policy. It assumes the choice itself causes the extra learning. If the difference were due to something else, such as motivation that made those students attend by choice, making attendance voluntary would not raise learning. Negate (2) and the argument collapses.

**10. Answer: (3)**
