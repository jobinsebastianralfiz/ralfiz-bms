# Practice set 2.2: Steps of research, sampling and tools

Time yourself: problems 1–8 in 11 minutes, problems 9–15 in 10 minutes. Every question has four options; choose one.

**1.** Arrange in the correct order: A. Determining the sample design B. Developing working hypotheses C. Extensive literature survey D. Preparing the research design E. Collecting the data
(1) C, B, D, A, E  (2) B, C, D, A, E  (3) C, D, B, A, E  (4) C, B, A, D, E

**2.** A sample of 50 is to be drawn systematically from a register of 2,000 households. If the random start is 13, the third household selected is number:
(1) 53  (2) 93  (3) 173  (4) 133

**3.** To study the problems of people who inject drugs, a researcher interviews one such person, who introduces two others, and so on. The sampling method is:
(1) Quota  (2) Cluster  (3) Snowball  (4) Systematic

**4.** Statement I: Lowering the level of significance from 0.05 to 0.01 reduces the probability of a Type I error.
Statement II: For a fixed sample size, lowering the level of significance also reduces the probability of a Type II error.
(1) Both Statement I and Statement II are true  (2) Both Statement I and Statement II are false  (3) Statement I is true but Statement II is false  (4) Statement I is false but Statement II is true

**5.** Match List I (tool) with List II (feature).

| List I | List II |
|---|---|
| A. Questionnaire | I. Mapping of choices and rejections within a group |
| B. Schedule | II. Filled in by the respondent |
| C. Sociometry | III. Anonymous rounds of expert opinion moving to consensus |
| D. Delphi technique | IV. Filled in by the enumerator |

(1) A-II, B-IV, C-I, D-III  (2) A-IV, B-II, C-I, D-III  (3) A-II, B-IV, C-III, D-I  (4) A-II, B-I, C-IV, D-III

**6.** The correlation between the two halves of a test is 0.50. Using the Spearman–Brown formula, first find the reliability of the full test, and then the reliability of a test three times as long as that full test. The second value is about:
(1) 0.75  (2) 0.67  (3) 0.86  (4) 0.90

**7.** In a study of the effect of yoga on examination anxiety, the researcher expects the effect to be stronger for first-year students than for final-year students. The year of study is a:
(1) Dependent variable  (2) Moderator variable  (3) Intervening variable  (4) Confounding variable that must be removed

**8.** Assertion (A): A schedule can be used with respondents who cannot read.
Reason (R): In a schedule, the enumerator reads out the questions and records the answers.
(1) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)  (2) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A)  (3) (A) is true but (R) is false  (4) (A) is false but (R) is true

**9.** Which of the following are non-sampling errors? A. Non-response B. An incomplete sampling frame C. The chance difference between a sample mean and the population mean D. Leading questions E. Data-entry mistakes
(1) C only  (2) A, B, D and E only  (3) A, C and D only  (4) B, C and E only

**10.** Which level of measurement allows the statement “Student X scored twice as many marks as student Y”?
(1) Nominal  (2) Ordinal  (3) Interval  (4) Ratio

**11.** A researcher reports: “The new aptitude test correlates 0.62 with first-year university grades obtained a year later.” This is evidence of:
(1) Content validity  (2) Concurrent validity  (3) Predictive validity  (4) Face validity

**12.** In a population of 3,000 students (2,400 in government schools and 600 in private schools), a proportionate stratified sample of 150 is drawn. The number of private-school students in the sample is:
(1) 50  (2) 30  (3) 75  (4) 120

**13.** Statement I: Each stratum should be as heterogeneous internally as possible.
Statement II: Each cluster should ideally be as heterogeneous internally as possible.
(1) Both Statement I and Statement II are true  (2) Both Statement I and Statement II are false  (3) Statement I is true but Statement II is false  (4) Statement I is false but Statement II is true

**14.** Two raters agree on 80 of 100 cases, and the agreement expected by chance is 0.60. Cohen’s kappa is:
(1) 0.80  (2) 0.20  (3) 0.33  (4) 0.50

**15.** Match List I (error or quantity) with List II (definition).

| List I | List II |
|---|---|
| A. Type I error | I. Probability of rejecting a false null hypothesis |
| B. Type II error | II. Rejecting a true null hypothesis |
| C. Power | III. Probability of a Type I error that the researcher accepts |
| D. Level of significance | IV. Failing to reject a false null hypothesis |

(1) A-IV, B-II, C-I, D-III  (2) A-II, B-IV, C-III, D-I  (3) A-II, B-I, C-IV, D-III  (4) A-II, B-IV, C-I, D-III
