# Practice set 1.3: Counting and recurrences

Time yourself: problems 1–8 in 12 minutes, problems 9–15 in 12 minutes. Every question has four options; choose one.

**1.** The number of distinct arrangements of the letters of COMMITTEE is:
(1) 45360  (2) 362880  (3) 90720  (4) 22680

**2.** A committee of 5 is chosen from 6 men and 4 women. The number of committees with at least 2 women is:
(1) 186  (2) 252  (3) 120  (4) 246

**3.** The number of positive integer solutions of x₁ + x₂ + x₃ + x₄ = 12 is:
(1) 455  (2) 165  (3) 220  (4) 330

**4.** The number of integer solutions of x₁ + x₂ + x₃ = 15 with 0 ≤ xᵢ ≤ 6 is:
(1) 136  (2) 10  (3) 1  (4) 28

**5.** The number of integers from 1 to 600 that are divisible by none of 2, 3 and 5 is:
(1) 440  (2) 140  (3) 160  (4) 200

**6.** The number of derangements of 7 objects is:
(1) 5040  (2) 1855  (3) 265  (4) 1854

**7.** The number of permutations of {1, 2, 3, 4, 5} with exactly one fixed point is:
(1) 44  (2) 45  (3) 20  (4) 120

**8.** If aₙ = 6aₙ₋₁ − 9aₙ₋₂ with a₀ = 1 and a₁ = 6, then a₃ is:
(1) 81  (2) 135  (3) 162  (4) 108

**9.** If aₙ = 3aₙ₋₁ + 4 with a₀ = 1, then a₄ is:
(1) 243  (2) 241  (3) 239  (4) 81

**10.** The number of distinct binary search trees that can be built from 6 distinct keys is:
(1) 720  (2) 132  (3) 429  (4) 64

**11.** A drawer holds 10 red, 8 blue and 6 green socks. The minimum number of socks to draw, without looking, to be sure of 3 socks of the same colour is:
(1) 4  (2) 6  (3) 7  (4) 9

**12.** Match List I with List II.

| List I | List II |
|---|---|
| A. Seatings of 6 people at a round table | I. 20 |
| B. Arrangements of 6 distinct books on a shelf | II. 28 |
| C. 3-member committees from 6 people | III. 120 |
| D. Ways to share 6 identical sweets among 3 children | IV. 720 |

(1) A-III, B-IV, C-II, D-I  (2) A-IV, B-III, C-I, D-II  (3) A-III, B-IV, C-I, D-II  (4) A-III, B-I, C-IV, D-II

**13.** Assertion (A): The number of ways to put 10 identical balls into 4 distinct boxes with no box empty is 84.
Reason (R): Putting one ball in each box first leaves 6 balls to distribute freely among 4 boxes, which can be done in C(6 + 3, 3) = 84 ways.
(1) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
(2) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A)
(3) (A) is true but (R) is false
(4) (A) is false but (R) is true

**14.** The coefficient of x⁸ in (x + x² + x³)⁴ is:
(1) 16  (2) 19  (3) 10  (4) 31
