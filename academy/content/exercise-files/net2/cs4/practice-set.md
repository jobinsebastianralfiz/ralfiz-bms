# Practice set 1.4: Induction and discrete probability

Time yourself: problems 1–8 in 12 minutes, problems 9–15 in 12 minutes. Every question has four options; choose one.

**1.** Two fair dice are rolled. Given that the sum is 8, what is the probability that both dice show even numbers?
(1) 3/5  (2) 1/4  (3) 2/5  (4) 5/36

**2.** A laboratory test detects a condition in 90% of people who have it and gives a false positive for 10% of people who do not. 20% of the tested group has the condition. A person tests positive. The probability that the person has the condition is:
(1) 0.90  (2) 0.26  (3) 9/13  (4) 0.18

**3.** A coin with P(heads) = 0.6 is tossed 4 times. The probability of exactly 2 heads is:
(1) 0.3456  (2) 0.36  (3) 0.1536  (4) 0.5

**4.** Five people are seated in a row at random. The probability that two particular people A and B sit next to each other is:
(1) 1/5  (2) 2/5  (3) 1/2  (4) 3/5

**5.** A random variable X has P(X = −1) = 0.3, P(X = 0) = 0.2, P(X = 2) = 0.5. Var(X) is:
(1) 0.7  (2) 2.3  (3) 1.81  (4) 1.51

**6.** On average, 3 typing errors occur per page (Poisson). The probability that a page has no error is (e^−3 ≈ 0.0498):
(1) 0.0498  (2) 0.1494  (3) 0.9502  (4) 0.2240

**7.** A and B are independent with P(A) = 0.4 and P(B) = 0.5. P(exactly one of A, B occurs) is:
(1) 0.2  (2) 0.9  (3) 0.5  (4) 0.7

**8.** A biased coin with P(heads) = 1/3 is tossed until the first head. The expected number of tosses is:
(1) 3  (2) 2  (3) 1.5  (4) 6

**9.** In the inductive step of proving 1 + 2 + … + n = n(n + 1)/2, after assuming the formula for n = k, the expression that must be shown equal to (k + 1)(k + 2)/2 is:
(1) k(k + 1)/2 + k  (2) k(k + 1)/2 + (k + 1)  (3) (k + 1)(k + 2)/2 + k  (4) k(k + 1) + (k + 1)

**11.** Assertion (A): If A and B are independent, then Ā and B̄ are also independent.
Reason (R): P(Ā ∩ B̄) = 1 − P(A ∪ B) = 1 − P(A) − P(B) + P(A)P(B) = (1 − P(A))(1 − P(B)).
(1) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
(2) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A)
(3) (A) is true but (R) is false
(4) (A) is false but (R) is true

**12.** Statement I: A proof by strong induction whose inductive step uses P(k) and P(k − 1) needs two base cases.
Statement II: The geometric distribution is memoryless.
(1) Both Statement I and Statement II are true
(2) Both Statement I and Statement II are false
(3) Statement I is true but Statement II is false
(4) Statement I is false but Statement II is true

**13.** Which of the following are true?

C. If A ⊆ B then P(B | A) = 1 (given P(A) > 0)
D. P(A | B) + P(Ā | B) = 1 (given P(B) > 0)

(1) A, B, C and D only  (2) A, B and D only  (3) A, C, D and E only  (4) B, C and D only

**14.** Three urns contain (red, white) balls: U₁ (1, 2), U₂ (2, 1), U₃ (3, 3). An urn is chosen with probabilities 1/2, 1/4, 1/4 and one ball is drawn. It is red. The probability that it came from U₂ is:
