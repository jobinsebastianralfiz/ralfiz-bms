# Practice set: Software process models (Lesson 6.1)

Time: 24 minutes. Each question carries 2 marks. There is no negative marking.

**1.** Match List I with List II.

| List I (Situation) | List II (Model) |
|---|---|
| A. The customer cannot describe the screens or reports they want | I. Spiral |
| B. A large defence system uses a new, untested sensor technology | II. Waterfall |
| C. A statutory tax module whose rules are fixed and well understood | III. Prototyping |
| D. A start-up expects to change features every two weeks from user feedback | IV. Scrum |

(1) A-III, B-I, C-II, D-IV (2) A-I, B-III, C-II, D-IV (3) A-III, B-II, C-I, D-IV (4) A-III, B-I, C-IV, D-II

**2.** Arrange the Unified Process milestones in the order in which they are reached.
A. Initial operational capability B. Life-cycle architecture C. Product release D. Life-cycle objectives

(1) D, A, B, C (2) D, B, A, C (3) B, D, A, C (4) D, B, C, A

**3.** Four risks are identified (loss in ₹ thousand): R1: P = 0.20, L = 900; R2: P = 0.50, L = 300; R3: P = 0.05, L = 4000; R4: P = 0.30, L = 400. Find the risk exposure of each, rank them, and give the total exposure.

**4.** For the risks in Problem 3, two mitigations are possible. M1 costs 50 and lowers the probability of R3 to 0.01. M2 costs 40 and lowers the probability of R1 to 0.05. Compute the RRL of each. Which should be funded first?

**5.** A backlog holds 240 points. The last four sprint velocities were 28, 32, 30 and 34. Sprints are 2 weeks long. (a) How many sprints and weeks are needed? (b) If the Product Owner adds 40 more points, how many sprints are needed?

**6.** An 8-person team is reorganised into two teams of 4, and only the two team leads talk across teams. Compare the number of communication paths before and after.

**7.** Assertion (A): The iterative waterfall model provides no feedback path to the feasibility study phase.
Reason (R): Once a project has been accepted after the feasibility study, the development team does not normally abandon it.
(1) Both true, R explains A (2) Both true, R does not explain A (3) A true, R false (4) A false, R true

**8.** Statement I: In the V-model, integration testing verifies the architectural (high-level) design.
Statement II: In the V-model, unit testing verifies the user requirements.
(1) Both true (2) Both false (3) I true, II false (4) I false, II true

**9.** Which of the following are principles behind the Agile Manifesto?
A. Welcome changing requirements, even late in development.
B. Working software is the primary measure of progress.
C. The most efficient way to convey information is through detailed written specifications.
D. Deliver working software frequently.
E. Maximise the amount of work done, including work that may not be needed.

(1) A, B and D only (2) A, B, C and D only (3) A, D and E only (4) B, C and D only

**10.** Arrange the classical waterfall phases in order: A. Design B. Maintenance C. Requirements analysis and specification D. Integration and system testing E. Feasibility study F. Coding and unit testing.

**11.** Each increment of a project takes 16 weeks, and a new increment starts every 8 weeks. There are 5 increments. When is the last increment delivered? How long would the project take if the increments were built one after another?

**12.** A 12-day sprint starts with 72 committed points. At the end of day 5, 40 points remain. Is the team ahead of or behind the ideal burndown line, and by how many points?

**13.** Which one of these is NOT a limitation of the RAD model, as described by Pressman?
(1) It needs enough people to form several RAD teams for large projects. (2) It is unsuitable if the system cannot be modularised. (3) It is risky when technical risks are high. (4) It cannot use reusable components.
